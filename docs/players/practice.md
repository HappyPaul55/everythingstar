Practice takes a stretch of a song and plays it at a speed you choose, as many times as you like. It is for the bar you keep dropping, the chorus you cannot sing yet, or the whole song at 70% before trying it at the record's speed.

Practice is a run with a plan, not a different game: the stage and the pause are the ordinary ones. What changes is where the run starts, where it stops and how fast the song plays.

## Open practice

Pause a run — <kbd>Escape</kbd>, or Start on a gamepad — and choose **Practice**. The pause menu also has **Practice this section**, which opens the same dialog with the stretch under the playhead already chosen.

Opening the dialog does not move the song. **Start practice** is the press that does.

## Choose a stretch

**Which stretch** lists what the song itself offers, in this order:

1. the song's own **sections** — `Intro`, `Verse 1`, `Chorus`, or whatever its source called them; an intro before the first section is a stretch too;
2. if the song has no sections, the first chart's **phrases**, numbered `Phrase 1` to `Phrase n`;
3. **Whole song**, when neither exists.

The list never reaches past the end of the song, and empty spans are left out rather than offered. A song with no length has no stretch to practise, and the dialog says so.

**Practice this section** reads the playhead at the press and seeds the dialog with the section it falls in, at full speed and without a loop. A moment before the first section falls back to the first choice.

## Speed

The speed is the **song's own playback speed**, from **50% to 100%** in steps of 10%. 100% is the record itself.

This is not the same as **Note speed** in Options. Note speed changes how much of the song the highway draws at once and never when a note is judged. Practice speed slows the song and the clock together: the notes arrive later because the music does. The pitch is kept, so a voice sounds like the singer rather than a slowed-down recording.

At 50% a fast passage is half as fast to read and to play. Judging windows move with the song, so the run is still the same game at a different tempo — not an easier one.

## Loop, or one pass

- **Loop this stretch** starts each pass again from the top of the stretch. Use it for a bar that needs twenty repetitions.
- With the loop off, **one pass** stops exactly at the end of the stretch and opens the pause.

A new pass starts clean, which is what a loop is for: the previous pass's voice or notes do not carry into the next, and a singer has to sing the top of the loop again.

## The count-in

There is no 3-2-1. A new run's stage entrance is its count-in: the room, then the highways and the words, then the song. A practice from the pause has already had that entrance, so it starts at once, at your chosen point rather than at the top of the song.

## It is not scored

Practice is not a scored run. It judges notes as you play — that is what the feedback is — but its total is not a run. A practice pass never reaches the results screen, cannot become a best, never appears on the **score board** and does not produce a brag card. The pause's read-out names the stretch so you know where you are, but nothing about a practice pass is saved.

That is deliberate: a partial pass from the middle of a song must never turn into a scoreable run. If you want the run to count, leave practice and play the song from the top.

## Stopping and leaving

Inside a practice pass, Pause offers the ordinary choices plus **Stop practice**.

- **Stop practice** clears the plan and starts the song again **from the top at 100%**, as an ordinary run. It does not resume from the middle.
- **Start again** repeats the stretch you chose.
- **Leave the stage** ends the run, clears the plan and takes you back to the songs. Nothing is saved.
- **Resume** carries on from where the pause landed, still inside the practice plan and at its speed.

A one-pass practice that reaches its end stops there and opens the pause, so the next press is a choice rather than a surprise.

## When to use it

- **A section you keep missing.** Loop it at 60–70%, then raise the speed a step at a time.
- **A song you have never played.** Practise the whole song at 80% to learn the shape before the record.
- **A singer learning a harmony.** Loop the part's line at a slower speed; the guide vocal can stay on while you learn it.
- **A drummer learning a fill.** Loop the phrase that contains it, with the fill's freestyle window in the same place each pass.

Practice uses the same band, controllers and settings as the run, so a mapping learned in the gear is the mapping you practise with. [Instruments](instruments.md) and [Singing](singing.md) cover the playing; [Options](options.md) has the settings that are not about the song.
