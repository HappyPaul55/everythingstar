Practice plays a stretch of a song at a speed you choose, as often as you like: the bar you keep dropping, the chorus you cannot sing yet, or the whole song at 70%. The stage and pause are ordinary; only the start, stop and speed change.

## Open practice

Pause a run — <kbd>Escape</kbd>, or Start on a gamepad — and choose **Practice**.

[The pause menu over the stage: resume, restart, practice and leave, with the quick panel of note speed and audio lag.](shot:pause)

The pause's **Practice this section** opens the same dialog with the stretch under the playhead chosen. Opening the dialog does not move the song; **Start practice** does. A practice from the pause has already had the stage's entrance, so it starts at once at your chosen point.

## Choose a stretch

[The practice dialog: the stretch to practise, its speed and the loop switch.](shot:practice-dialog)

**Which stretch** lists what the song offers, in this order:

1. its own **sections** — `Intro`, `Verse 1`, `Chorus`, or whatever its source called them; an intro before the first section is a stretch too;
2. if it has no sections, the first chart's **phrases**, numbered `Phrase 1` to `Phrase n`;
3. **Whole song**, when neither exists.

The list never reaches past the end of the song, and empty spans are left out. A song with no length has no stretch, and the dialog says so.

**Practice this section** reads the playhead at the press and seeds the dialog with that section, at full speed and without a loop; before the first section it falls back to the first choice.

## Speed

The speed is the **song's own playback speed**, from **50% to 100%** in steps of 10%, where 100% is the record.

This is not **Note speed** in Options, which changes how much of the song is drawn and never when a note is judged. Practice speed slows the song and clock together, so notes arrive later because the music does; the pitch is kept, so a voice still sounds like the singer. At 50% a fast passage is half as fast to read and play, and the judging windows move too: the same game at another tempo, not an easier one.

[A practice run at 80%: the stage's read-out naming the stretch, its speed and the loop.](shot:practice-readout)

## Loop, or one pass

- **Loop this stretch** starts each pass again from the top of the stretch, for a bar that needs twenty repetitions.
- With the loop off, **one pass** stops exactly at the end of the stretch and opens the pause.

A new pass starts clean: the previous pass's voice or notes do not carry into the next, so a singer has to sing the top of the loop again.

## It is not scored

Practice judges notes as you play, but its total is not a run: a pass never reaches the results, cannot become a best, never appears on the **score board** and does not produce a brag card. Nothing about a pass is saved, so a partial run from the middle of a song cannot become a scoreable one. If you want it to count, leave practice and play from the top.

## Stopping and leaving

Inside a practice pass, Pause offers the ordinary choices plus **Stop practice**.

- **Stop practice** clears the plan and starts the song again **from the top at 100%**, as an ordinary run; it does not resume from the middle.
- **Start again** repeats the stretch you chose.
- **Leave the stage** ends the run, clears the plan and returns to the songs. Nothing is saved.
- **Resume** carries on from where the pause landed, still inside the plan and at its speed.

A one-pass practice stops at its end and opens the pause, so the next press is a choice rather than a surprise.

## When to use it

Loop a section you keep missing at 60–70% and raise the speed a step at a time; a whole song at 80% teaches its shape. For a harmony, loop the part slower with the guide vocal on; for a fill, loop the phrase so its freestyle window lands in the same place each pass.

Practice uses the same band, controllers and settings as the run, so a mapping learned in the gear is the one you practise with. [Instruments](instruments.md) and [Singing](singing.md) cover the playing; [Options](options.md) has the settings not about the song.
