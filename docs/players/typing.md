Typing is the words on your keyboard. On a pitched song a words player can choose **sing** or **Type**; on a song with no pitch, typing is the only way to play the words. You type each word as it is due, and a typist keeps their own score, combo and run.

## What you see

The words sit on the melody region above the instrument panels. On a pitched song with a singer they ride under the note bars, and a typist reads whole words in the line strips below; with no singer they fill the region in the lyrics view and do not scroll.

[The typing view: the song's words filling the melody region, with the word you are on highlighted and the next lines waiting.](shot:typing)

The word you are on is marked character by character: typed letters are done, the next is the caret, the rest are still to come. A finished word moves the run on at once.

## The three levels

Only what counts towards a word changes with the level.

- **Easy** wants the **first character** of each word: `Tonight` is `t`, and the rest is yours to ignore.
- **Normal** wants the **whole word**, with punctuation and accents optional: `Tonight!` is `tonight` and `Café` is `cafe`. Punctuation and symbols are not typed; letters and digits are.
- **Hard** wants the **whole word with its punctuation and accents**, so `Tonight!` needs the `!` and `Café` the `é`. Spaces are never typed.

Nothing is case-sensitive at any level, so Shift is never a wrong answer.

**What the band screen asks.** Typing has its own three levels, separate from an instrument's four: nobody types an Expert chart. A typist plays at **Normal**, called **typed — free play** on the band screen for a pitched and a lyrics-only song alike; the level buttons there are the song's charts, not a typing level.

## The forgiving rules

Typing is not a spelling test with a buzzer. Three rules keep it fair:

- **A key can skip ahead.** If a key cannot continue the word you are on but begins a later open word, you jump to that word. The words passed over are missed and your chain breaks, but the run carries on. The search runs to the end of the line and then to the first word of the next, so a word's tail or a line's end cannot trap you.
- **A line opens early and holds late.** A line can be typed from up to a second before its first word, but never before the previous line has finished. Its last word stays typeable for up to a second into the gap after it, or until the next line begins, whichever comes first.
- **A line opens together.** Only the line's own timing is real, so once a line opens, every word in it can be typed, and a fast typist is never blocked part-way through.

## What counts as a mistake

- **A wrong key** is an error. The word stays and what you had typed of it is cleared, so you can try again; the error is counted.
- **A skip** is an error on the key that made it, and the words passed over are misses.
- **A word whose time runs out** is a miss, not an error: nobody did anything. A miss resets your combo.
- **A key that means nothing at the level** is ignored rather than counted. At Normal a full stop is a key the word has no use for; punctuation and symbols are what Easy and Normal ignore.
- **<kbd>Backspace</kbd> is free.** It edits what you have typed and is never counted, and neither is a key pressed before its word opens.

Control, Command and Alt combinations go to the browser — <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>R</kbd> still reloads — and a key that is not a single character, such as an arrow or <kbd>F5</kbd>, is not a typing key. An unfinished input-method composition is ignored until it arrives.

## Words and score

A word is worth **50 points**, a **hero** word five times its own, and the streak multiplier is ×1, ×2, ×3 and ×4 at 0, 10, 20 and 30 words. A miss resets the chain; words are judged on being completed in their window, not on typing speed within it.

## The read-out at the end

A typist's results card carries two numbers from the run's own keystrokes:

- **Words a minute** — five characters to a word, over the span from the first meaningful key to the last, with a one-second floor so two quick keys cannot read as thousands of words a minute.
- **Key accuracy** — the share of meaningful keys that were right. Correct and wrong characters count; <kbd>Backspace</kbd>, ignored keys and keys before a word opened do not.

A typist who never touched the keyboard has no rate rather than a zero. Neither number is used in judging.

## Pitched songs and lyrics-only songs

On a pitched song both ways read the same words and keep independent scores. On a **lyrics-only** song there is no pitch and so no singer score: the words are typed in free play and **Listen** is the unscored read-along. [Singing](singing.md) covers the singer's side.

## More than one typist

Each typist has their own run and score. One keyboard belongs to one typist: with two typists, the one the band gives the keyboard types and the others are played by the machine.

A typist and an instrument player can share the keyboard, because one types characters and the other presses keys — but every letter is also a key an instrument may have bound, so a gamepad or MIDI kit is cleaner for both.
